PROMPT:
A client invested Rs 5,00,000 in Stock A and Rs 3,00,000 in Stock B on the same day. After 6 months, Stock A is up 12% and Stock B is down 8%. What is the client's total profit or loss in rupees, and what is the percentage return on the total portfolio?

Think step by step and show your working:
Step 1: Calculate Stock A profit.
Step 2: Calculate Stock B loss.
Step 3: Calculate net profit.
Step 4: State total amount invested.
Step 5: Calculate portfolio return percentage.

MODEL RESPONSE (step by step, much more accurate):
To find the client's total profit or loss and the percentage return on the total portfolio, we'll follow the steps outlined.

Step 1: Calculate Stock A profit.
Stock A's initial investment: Rs 5,00,000
Stock A's percentage gain: 12%
Stock A's profit = 12% of Rs 5,00,000 = 0.12 * 5,00,000 = Rs 60,000

Step 2: Calculate Stock B loss.
Stock B's initial investment: Rs 3,00,000
Stock B's percentage loss: 8%
Stock B's loss = 8% of Rs 3,00,000 = 0.08 * 3,00,000 = Rs 24,000

Step 3: Calculate net profit.
Net profit = Stock A's profit - Stock B's loss = Rs 60,000 - Rs 24,000 = Rs 36,000

Step 4: State total amount invested.
Total amount invested = Stock A's investment + Stock B's investment = Rs 5,00,000 + Rs 3,00,000 = Rs 8,00,000

Step 5: Calculate portfolio return percentage.
Portfolio return percentage = (Net profit / Total amount invested) * 100
= (Rs 36,000 / Rs 8,00,000) * 100
= 4.5%

So, the client's total profit is Rs 36,000, and the percentage return on the total portfolio is 4.5%.
