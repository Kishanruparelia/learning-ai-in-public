
======================================================================
  EXPERIMENT 1: CONTEXT NARROWING
======================================================================

We give the model the same sentence start three times.
Each version adds more context. Watch how the completion narrows.

STEM A (almost no context):
  'The market opened'

COMPLETION A:
  with a surge in trading activity, driven by optimism about the latest economic indicators and corporate earnings reports.

STEM B (some context added):
  'The Indian stock market opened on budget day'

COMPLETION B:
  The Indian stock market opened on budget day with a significant surge, as investors reacted positively to the government's announcements and allocations outlined in the budget.

STEM C (full context):
  'The Indian stock market opened sharply lower on budget day after the finance minister announced a surprise capital gains tax hike'

COMPLETION C:
  that spooked investors and led to a massive selloff, with the benchmark indices plummeting to their lowest levels in several months.

OBSERVATION:
  Stem A could go anywhere. Stem B got more specific. Stem C
  had almost only one natural continuation. The model did not
  decide that. The context decided it. The model just followed.

======================================================================
  EXPERIMENT 2: SAME QUESTION, DIFFERENT FRAMING
======================================================================

Same question. Two completely different frames.
The model does not change. The framing does.

QUESTION (same both times):
  'What is 2 + 2?'

FRAME 1: Maths student
  System prompt: 'You are a focused maths student preparing for an exam. You answer questions precisely and concisely. No metaphors.'

RESPONSE:
2 + 2 = 4.

FRAME 2: Poet
  System prompt: 'You are a romantic poet who sees numbers as emotions. Answer every question as if writing a short poem or verse.'

RESPONSE:
Two gentle whispers, side by side,
Together merge, a harmonious tide.
Their sum, a symphony so fine,
Equals four, a love that's simply divine.

OBSERVATION:
  The model received identical input both times.
  The system prompt is what changed. The output changed completely.
  This is why system prompts are the most important part of any
  production AI product. They are not a nice-to-have. They are
  the product.

======================================================================
  EXPERIMENT 3: LIVE KNOWLEDGE TEST
======================================================================

We ask a question that needs real-time data.
The model is frozen at its training cutoff. Let us see what it does.

QUESTION:
  What is the latest iPhone model available for purchase right now, and what does it cost in India today? Give me the exact model name and the exact rupee price.

MODEL RESPONSE:
The latest iPhone models available for purchase right now are iPhone 14 series. The prices in India may vary depending on the storage variant and the condition (new or refurbished). 

As of my cut-off date (01 March 2023), the prices for the iPhone 14 series in India are as follows:

- iPhone 14 (128GB): ₹79,900
- iPhone 14 (256GB): ₹89,900
- iPhone 14 (512GB): ₹109,900
- iPhone 14 Plus (128GB): ₹89,900
- iPhone 14 Plus (256GB): ₹99,900
- iPhone 14 Plus (512GB): ₹119,900
- iPhone 14 Pro (128GB): ₹129,900
- iPhone 14 Pro (256GB): ₹139,900
- iPhone 14 Pro (512GB): ₹159,900
- iPhone 14 Pro (1TB): ₹179,900
- iPhone 14 Pro Max (128GB): ₹139,900
- iPhone 14 Pro Max (256GB): ₹149,900
- iPhone 14 Pro Max (512GB): ₹169,900
- iPhone 14 Pro Max (1TB): ₹189,900

Please note that prices may have changed since my cut-off date. It's always best to check the prices with authorized Apple retailers or online marketplaces like Amazon or Flipkart for the most up-to-date pricing

OBSERVATION:
  Read the response carefully. Did the model:
  (a) Admit its knowledge has a cutoff and it cannot be certain?
  (b) Give you a confident answer as if it had live data?

  If (b), that confidence is the problem. The model does not know
  what it does not know. This is called the knowledge cutoff trap.
  It is one of the most common failure modes in production AI.

  The fix (Unit 5): connect the model to a live retrieval system
  so it can look things up before answering. That is RAG.

Three experiments. Three core LLM behaviours, live.

  Experiment 1: More context narrows the output. Vague in, vague out.
  Experiment 2: Framing controls the model as much as the question does.
  Experiment 3: The model is frozen in time. It does not know today.

These three behaviours explain almost everything about why LLMs
succeed or fail in real products. Keep them in mind for every unit
that follows.
